Senior software engineer · Seattle

Ben Whiting

During the week I build products, and the platforms under them, for places where failing is expensive: power substations, stadium security, defense. On weekends I build things that only need to make it to Monday. This is where the weekend ones end up.

Browse my work →

I’ve been writing software for more than a decade, on both ends of a product’s life: 0 → 1, taking an idea to a first working version, and 1 → n, turning that version into something a lot of people depend on. Along the way I’ve built the part users see and what it stands on, from AWS infrastructure and Kubernetes platforms to delivery pipelines and the machinery that gets machine learning models out of research and into production.

Several of those first versions became real products. I designed the prototype that became SEL Blueframe, a container platform for electrical substations, and later led it. At Amazon I worked on Alexa Smart Home and led the design of the developer recommendation system that became Skill Quality Coach. At Pacific Northwest National Laboratory I was a tech lead on production AI and co-developed the real-time video analytics in Guardian-iiS, a multi-camera security platform that was deployed at Super Bowl LX. These days I’m a forward deployed engineer at Vannevar, a defense technology company. The full history is on LinkedIn.

Outside of work I’m a weekend project warrior: I pick something that looks finishable on a Saturday morning and build it because it sounds fun. So far that has produced a ramen shop for yokai and a viking that sets fire to web pages, among other things. The catalog has all of them, and each entry says what the project is, what was fiddly about building it, and where to try it.
